summary: >-
  Lake Malawi water is soft (about 3 to 5 dGH) but alkaline (pH 7.7 to 8.6) and buffered by
  bicarbonate, much of it balanced by sodium, so its carbonate hardness of 5 to 8 dKH exceeds its
  total hardness. Aquarium care sheets recommend far harder water, and cichlids.com species profiles
  therefore record the measured lake range and, separately, a wider tolerance range that contains
  it.

## What the lake water contains

Lake Malawi is warm, low in minerals and alkaline. Its surface water warms to about 28 °C in summer and cools to below 24 °C in the windier, cooler winter[^vollmer-2005]. Early 1960s measurements put the pH at 7.7 to 8.6[^fao-malawi], and conductivity in the upper 80 m stayed between 248 and 258 µS/cm from 1976 to 1997[^branchu-2010].

Profiles from the north basin, sampled in 1993, average about 19 mg/L calcium, 7.4 mg/L magnesium, 20.6 mg/L sodium and 6.4 mg/L potassium, with an alkalinity of 2.4 meq/L, barely changing with depth[^branchu-2010]. The 1960s analyses agree: 19.8 mg/L calcium, 21 mg/L sodium and 144 mg/L bicarbonate plus carbonate, against about 4 mg/L chloride and at most 5.5 mg/L sulfate[^fao-malawi]. In aquarium units this is about 4.4 dGH and 6.7 dKH, since one dGH and one dKH both stand for 0.357 meq/L[^branchu-2010][^aqion-hardness]. The carbonate hardness is the larger number.

## Why the carbonate hardness exceeds the total hardness

GH counts only calcium and magnesium. KH is the alkalinity found by acid titration, and it includes every bicarbonate and carbonate ion, whichever cation balances it[^aqion-hardness][^boyd-2007]. In Lake Malawi, calcium and magnesium provide about 1.55 meq/L of positive charge, the bicarbonate about 2.4 meq/L. Sodium and potassium add about 1.06 meq/L, enough for the 0.85 meq/L of bicarbonate left over plus the little chloride and sulfate[^branchu-2010].

When alkalinity is larger than hardness, part of the bicarbonate must belong to sodium and potassium[^kutty-1987]. The aqion reference calls this share of the KH pseudo hardness and notes that nearly all natural waters show the reverse, with GH at least as high as KH[^aqion-hardness]. In Lake Malawi it amounts to roughly 2.4 dKH[^branchu-2010][^aqion-hardness].

Branchu and co-authors trace the composition mainly to weathering of ancient granitic and metamorphic rock in the catchment, concentrated about threefold by evaporation; in the north, volcanic rock and sodium bicarbonate hot springs add alkalinity[^branchu-2010]. Boyd explains that dissolving limestone supplies hardness and alkalinity in matching amounts, whereas feldspars release bicarbonate without hardness[^boyd-2007]. A catchment of silicate rock can therefore yield more KH than GH. The surface water is also slightly supersaturated with calcite, aragonite and dolomite, so a carbonate mineral caps its calcium and magnesium[^branchu-2010].

## Why care sheets list much harder water

On the water engineering scale, up to 75 mg/L as CaCO3 counts as soft[^kutty-1987]; Lake Malawi has 61 to 86 mg/L and sits at that boundary[^talling-table]. Care sheets ask for far more: the OATA care sheet gives 12 to 18 dGH and 10 to 15 dKH[^oata], the Seriously Fish profile of the red zebra 10 to 25 dGH[^seriously-fish]. That is two to six times the lake's hardness.

None of the sources consulted explains these values, but two hobby habits may contribute. In the cichlids.com forum archive, keepers call Malawi water "hard" when they mean alkaline and give hardness targets in dKH[^archive-15920][^archive-53942]; one post even suggests Epsom salt, which adds GH and no alkalinity, to raise dKH[^archive-13729][^aqion-hardness]. And the classic calcium carbonate buffers raise GH together with KH[^boyd-2007].

## How cichlids.com profiles handle the gap

Care-sheet pH ranges fit the lake: 7.6 to 8.8 spans its 7.7 to 8.6, and 8.0 to 8.6 lies inside it. The hardness ranges do not: 10 to 25 and 12 to 18 dGH lie wholly above the lake's 3 to 5 dGH, and 10 to 15 dKH above its 5 to 8 dKH[^seriously-fish][^oata][^branchu-2010][^fao-malawi].

cichlids.com species profiles therefore keep two ranges per water value. The natural range is the value measured in the habitat, for lake endemics the lake value, and care sheets never count as its source. The tolerance range is a separate value, decided by vote from husbandry literature, care sheets, breeders and the forum archive, and defined to contain the natural range. For the red zebra this gives the lake's 3 to 5 dGH as natural range and 3 to 25 dGH, up to the highest care-sheet value, as tolerance[^branchu-2010][^fao-malawi][^seriously-fish]. Seachem recommends 4 to 8 dGH for Malawi tanks, close to the lake[^seachem-lake-salt], and one keeper in the archive holds that Malawi cichlids do not need very hard water[^archive-13729].

## What the classic buffers do

Coral sand, crushed coral, shells and limestone are calcium carbonate[^archive-23992][^archive-61857]. One keeper reports that coral slowly lifts the pH to as much as 8.6[^archive-23992]; calcite dissolving in pure water open to the air settles lower, at pH 8.2 with about 3 dGH and 3 dKH[^aqion-calcite]. Each dKH from calcium carbonate arrives with one dGH, so these materials raise pH and KH but cannot create the lake's surplus of KH over GH[^boyd-2007][^aqion-hardness].

Sodium bicarbonate (baking soda) does: a 1 mmol/L solution has 1 meq/L of alkalinity, no hardness and pH 8.3[^aqion-hardness]. Keepers reach about pH 8.2 with it[^archive-13729], but warn that it is a weak, short-lived buffer and keep coral in the substrate as well[^archive-17725][^archive-23992].

Seachem splits the job: its Malawi/Victoria Buffer of carbonate salts raises KH and holds the pH at 7.8 to 8.4, by the maker's account without touching GH[^seachem-buffer], while its Cichlid Lake Salt supplies calcium, magnesium, sodium and potassium and changes hardness only[^seachem-lake-salt]. Tropic Marin Pro-Cichlid Mineral is sold to raise pH and mineral content; its label names only sodium and potassium salts plus trace elements, so it should add buffer without GH, although the maker does not say so[^tropic-marin][^aqion-hardness].

## What follows for the aquarium

The lake shows the water Malawi cichlids come from: soft, alkaline and well buffered[^branchu-2010][^fao-malawi]. No source found here ranks a stable pH against an exact GH. Keepers in the archive value a steady pH of about 8.0 to 8.4[^archive-49931], and with the lake at 3 to 5 dGH it seems reasonable, though untested, to watch pH and KH more closely than GH within the tolerance range[^branchu-2010][^fao-malawi].

Filter bacteria that oxidise ammonia to nitrate release acid and consume alkalinity, and with heavy feeding alkalinity and pH can fall fast[^boyd-nitrification]. Falling pH and rising nitrate thus share one cause. The lake holds about 0.02 mg/L nitrate nitrogen, about 0.09 mg/L expressed as nitrate (NO3)[^fao-malawi]; OATA allows at most 20 mg/L nitrate above the tap water level, with 25 % of the water changed weekly[^oata].

- Soft tap water: add buffer with every water change. One keeper turns tap water of pH 7.2, 2 dGH and 2 dKH into pH 8.2, 7 dKH and 13 dGH with Epsom salt, baking soda, table salt and trace elements over crushed coral[^archive-17725]; less Epsom salt would keep the GH nearer the lake[^aqion-hardness].
- Hard tap water: test first. Seachem advises measuring GH before adding lake salt and notes that hard source water shifts the result of its buffer[^seachem-lake-salt][^seachem-buffer].

## Lake Malawi compared with Tanganyika and Victoria

Each range merges several limnological studies: for Lake Malawi[^branchu-2010][^fao-malawi][^vollmer-2005][^eccles-1974][^ngochera-2018], for Lake Tanganyika[^fao-tanganyika][^plisnier-1996][^plisnier-1996b][^verburg-1997][^cohen-1997] and for Lake Victoria[^fao-victoria][^burgis-1987][^ochumba-1987][^orina-2020][^makaka-2021][^bcodmo-winam].

| | Lake Malawi | Lake Tanganyika | Lake Victoria |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature | 21 to 29 °C | 23 to 29 °C | 22 to 30 °C |
| pH | 7.7 to 8.6 | 8.4 to 9.4 | 6.8 to 9.4 |
| Total hardness | 3 to 5 dGH | 10 to 13 dGH | 1 to 3 dGH |
| Carbonate hardness | 5 to 8 dKH | 15 to 20 dKH | 2 to 6 dKH |
| Conductivity | 240 to 260 µS/cm | 520 to 730 µS/cm | 80 to 180 µS/cm |

All three lakes have more KH than GH[^fao-tanganyika][^fao-victoria][^branchu-2010]. Lake Tanganyika is hard, mostly through magnesium: 9.8 mg/L calcium, 43.3 mg/L magnesium and 6.71 meq/L alkalinity give about 11 dGH and 19 dKH[^fao-tanganyika], at about 660 µS/cm and pH 9.0 near the surface[^plisnier-1996]. Open Lake Victoria has 5.6 mg/L calcium, 2.6 mg/L magnesium and 55 mg/L bicarbonate plus carbonate, about 1.4 dGH and 2.5 dKH[^fao-victoria]; single samples from the shallow Winam Gulf ranged from pH 6.7 to 9.0 in 2022 and 2023[^bcodmo-winam]. Lake Malawi lies between them, closer to Victoria[^talling-table].

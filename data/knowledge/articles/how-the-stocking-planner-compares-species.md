## What the planner knows about each species

Every species in the catalog has the same features, each with a value, its sources and a confidence:

- water values (temperature, pH, GH, KH), each as a natural range measured in the habitat and a tolerance range that contains it;
- maximum size and a predation level from 0 (eats no fish) to 3 (a pronounced fish eater);
- diet type (aufwuchs grazer, omnivore, invertivore, piscivore) with a flag for species prone to bloat;
- aggression towards its own and towards other species, each from 0 to 4;
- territory, habitat zones, social structure with a flag for a species-only tank, hybrid group, coloration, feeding speed, minimum tank and origin.

Aggression has two values because a species like Tropheus moorii quarrels with its own kind and is fairly calm towards other fish[^sf-tropheus][^konings-2009].

## Why a single similarity score fails

An overall similarity score would rate close relatives as ideal partners, yet they are often the worst choice. Males of the mbuna Metriaclima mbenjii attacked opponents with a colour like their own more often, whatever their species[^dijkstra-2011], and in the laboratory even fairly distant mbuna can produce fertile hybrids[^genner-2005].

Water ranges have to overlap, which an average cannot express. Predation works one way: in northern pike, mouth width limited the size of prey, and prey body depth decided what could be swallowed[^nilsson-2000]; the planner applies this principle to cichlids. Tank size matters too: Seriously Fish expects Midas cichlid aggression to ease in very large tanks, above about 1000 litres[^sf-midas].

Each feature of a pair therefore gets its own conflict function with a value from 0 to 1: overlap for natural and tolerance ranges, a conflict matrix for diet, mouth size for predation, the difference in aggression and territory (softened by tank size and habitat zones), similarity of genus, hybrid group and coloration, and origin with a low weight. A fish counts as prey when its maximum size lies below one third of the predator's for predation levels 2 and 3, and below 2 cm for level 1.

## How a conflict becomes a hint, a caution or a warning

None of the three levels blocks a combination. A hint is worth knowing, a caution depends on conditions such as tank length, and a warning marks a combination known to be problematic. A hardness class caps the level that a conflict in one feature reaches on its own:

- hard, up to a warning: tolerance ranges without overlap, predation, a shared hybrid group, a species-only recommendation, a bloat-prone aufwuchs grazer next to a meat eater;
- medium, up to a caution: aggression towards other species, territory, natural ranges without overlap, minimum tank;
- soft, no level of their own: coloration, origin (medium in a biotope tank), feeding speed, habitat zone.

Soft features add to the sum and make other conflicts weigh more, as with two territorial species of the same colour: in many haplochromine cichlids, though not all, males attack rivals of similar colour more often[^dijkstra-2011].

A pair gets the higher of two levels: one from the weighted sum of all conflicts, one from the strongest single conflict within its hardness cap, so good matches elsewhere never hide a serious conflict. Tank checks add volume and footprint per species, group sizes, total stocking and rules such as Tropheus species-only. Every message gives both species' values and the deciding features, links each value to its source and, where possible, suggests a way out.

## Natural range and tolerance

The natural range is what was measured where the species lives. The tolerance range is where it stays healthy for years according to literature and keeping experience. Natural ranges without overlap are a medium conflict, since at least one fish then lives in water unlike its home; tolerances without overlap are a hard one.

The two can lie far apart. Lake Malawi measured 15.1 to 20.2 mg/L calcium, 6 to 9 mg/L magnesium, pH 7.7 to 8.6 and an alkalinity of 2.0 to 2.59 meq/L[^fao-malawi]. That gives about 3.4 to 4.8 dGH of total hardness but 5.6 to 7.3 dKH of carbonate hardness, which here exceeds the total hardness[^aqion-hardness]. Seriously Fish recommends 10 to 25 dGH for Labidochromis caeruleus[^sf-caeruleus]. Since care sheets can blur hardness and alkalinity, natural ranges come from field measurements, and GH and KH stay separate values.

## Where the values come from

One agent researches every feature with sources and reasoning; two more, with other models or prompts, determine the same values blind. On medium and soft features, an adjudicating agent settles differences with a stated reason.

Predation, diet, hybrid group, the species-only flag, every tolerance and the natural water values of species outside the lake tables are voted on. A value is settled when all three votes agree or, after tie-breaker agents add votes, when four of five agree, and only if the agreeing votes bring at least two distinct external sources (catalogs, literature or databases). A script counts the votes, so each decision is repeatable.

Otherwise the most cautious value applies with low confidence: the highest predation level, species-only if any vote says so, the most common named hybrid group, the strictest diet type with the bloat flag if any vote sets it, and for ranges the intersection of the votes, or their median range when the intersection is empty. A cautious tolerance is widened to contain the natural range. Such values trigger more messages, the safer error for the fish.

Species of Lakes Malawi, Tanganyika and Victoria take their natural water values from lake measurements; care sheets count only for tolerances. The species card shows, for every value, what each source states side by side.

## Five test combinations

### Tropheus moorii with Amphilophus citrinellus: warning

Tropheus tear algae from rocks[^konings-2009], and among Tanganyika cichlids those lowest in the food chain have the longest guts[^wagner-2009]. Soft, protein-rich food can kill Tropheus[^konings-2009][^tsi-tropheus]. Bloat is not fully understood; it appears more often with too much or poor food, and many experienced keepers suspect a gut flagellate[^pfk-tropheus-2026]. Meaty food for a predatory tankmate overloads Tropheus with protein, and Practical Fishkeeping favours a tank for Tropheus alone[^pfk-tropheus-2026].

The Midas cichlid tries to eat nearly anything, takes smaller fish in the wild, ranks among the most aggressive cichlids and is fed earthworms, prawns and mussels besides vegetables[^sf-midas]. The species-only rule alone can reach a warning; aggression adds a medium conflict. How much such an omnivore weighs against a bloat-prone grazer in the diet matrix is part of the open calibration.

### Maylandia estherae with Maylandia zebra: warning

The red zebra (Pseudotropheus estherae at Seriously Fish) was taken for a colour form of M. zebra until its description in 1995[^sf-estherae]. In laboratory tests, males of M. zebra and the related 'gold zebra' seemed unable to tell the females of the two apart; with a free choice of mates these species did not interbreed, but females held only with males of the other kind bore fertile hybrids[^genner-2005]. Both species share a hybrid group in the catalog, a hard conflict, so the planner warns although water, diet and habitat match.

### Cyphotilapia frontosa with Neolamprologus multifasciatus: warning

Frontosa reaches about 25 cm standard length and hunts smaller fish[^sf-frontosa]; isotope models of Cyphotilapia in the lake rank mussels, jellyfish and fish as its main foods, in shares that vary by site[^jackson-2026]. N. multifasciatus reaches 4.5 cm standard length[^sf-multifasciatus], far below a third of the frontosa's size (about 8 cm), the prey threshold of a level 2 predator. The one-sided conflict is hard: warning.

### Discus with Malawi cichlids: warning expected

Discus live in Amazon floodplains of white and black water[^crampton-2008]; rivers of the Rio Negro basin, mostly black water, measured pH 3.9 to 6.1, the white-water Solimões 6.9 to 7.0[^kuchler-2000], against pH 7.7 to 8.6 in Lake Malawi[^fao-malawi]. Care sheets give discus pH 5.0 to 7.0[^sf-discus], L. caeruleus 7.7 to 8.6[^sf-caeruleus].

The catalog's natural ranges of discus and L. caeruleus are far apart: pH 5.2 to 6.9 against 7.7 to 8.6, GH 0 to 2 against 3 to 5 dGH, KH 0 to 2 against 5 to 8 dKH, three medium conflicts. The voted tolerances overlap only at their edges: pH 4.8 to 7.5 against 7.2 to 8.8, GH 0 to 10 against 3 to 25, KH 0 to 5 against 5 to 12. The hard rule for tolerances without overlap thus does not apply as such; how a mere edge overlap counts is part of the open calibration, and the test expects a warning.

### Labidochromis caeruleus with Cyrtocara moorii: no message

Both are endemic to Lake Malawi[^sf-caeruleus][^sf-cyrtocara]. L. caeruleus lives among rocks and Vallisneria and suits peaceful haplochromines[^sf-caeruleus]. C. moorii, a calm fish of shallow sand, trails sand diggers for what they uncover and can scare small fish by its bulk; Seriously Fish advises against mbuna as its tankmates in general[^sf-cyrtocara]. At about 10 cm, L. caeruleus[^sf-caeruleus] is far above the 2 cm prey threshold of C. moorii, a level 1 predator in the catalog. No feature produces a conflict strong enough for a message, and the planner shows none.

## How the weights are set

The weights are first set by hand from expert knowledge, then matched against known good and bad combinations and later against real tanks in the app whose stocking stays stable for a long time; the detailed conflict functions, starting weights and thresholds are open. Domain unit tests, among them these five pairs, check the expected level and that the reason names the expected features, and every change to features, weights or thresholds must pass them.
